Housing

Student Housing Assistance in France

Moving to France for university? FranceMate helps international students understand student housing, rental documents, insurance, and housing procedures.

Finding Student Accommodation

Finding housing in France can be highly competitive, especially in major cities like Paris, Lyon, and Toulouse. Here are the main types of housing available for students:

  • CROUS: Public student residences. Highly affordable but very competitive; usually reserved for scholarship holders, but international students can apply during specific phases.
  • Private Student Residences: Fully furnished apartments specifically for students, often offering amenities like gyms, laundry, and study rooms.
  • Private Rental (Location): Renting directly from a landlord or via an agency.
  • Colocation (Flatsharing): Renting a room in a larger apartment shared with other students or young professionals.
  • Airbnb/Short-term: Often used as a temporary solution while searching for permanent housing after arriving in France.

Documents Landlords May Request

In France, a comprehensive housing dossier (dossier de location) is essential. Landlords typically ask for:

Identity & Status

Passport, visa or carte de sjour, and proof of student status (attestation d'inscription or student ID).

Financial Proof

Proof of sufficient income or a scholarship certificate.

Guarantor Documents

If using a guarantor, their ID, tax returns, and recent payslips.

Rental Terminology You Should Know

  • Contrat de location (Bail): The lease agreement.
  • Caution (Dpt de garantie): Security deposit, usually equal to 1-2 months' rent without charges.
  • Charges comprises (CC): Rent price including building maintenance and sometimes water/heating.
  • tat des lieux: Inventory of fixtures done upon moving in and out. Crucial for getting your deposit back!
  • DPE: Energy performance certificate of the apartment.

Housing Insurance & Guarantors

Housing Insurance (Assurance Habitation)

In France, it is mandatory to have housing insurance before you receive the keys to your rental. It covers risks like fire, water damage, and burglary.

Guarantor (Garant)

Most landlords require a guarantorsomeone who promises to pay your rent if you cannot. International students often use Visale (a free state guarantee by Action Logement) or private guarantor services like Garantme.

CAF / Housing Assistance

The Caisse d'Allocations Familiales (CAF) provides financial housing assistance (APL or ALS) to eligible students, including international students. You can apply for this subsidy after moving into your apartment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Wiring money before signing a lease or seeing the apartment (high risk of scams).
  • Rushing the tat des lieux (inventory of fixtures) upon move-in.
  • Forgetting to set up electricity/gas and internet contracts.
  • Not applying for CAF as soon as possible.
