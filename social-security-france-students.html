Social Security in France for International Students

Understanding health coverage and navigating registration with CPAM.

What is French Social Security?

French Social Security (ScuritSociale, also known through CPAM or Assurance Maladie) is the national healthcare system in France. For students, registration means a significant portion of medical expensessuch as doctor's visits, pharmacy prescriptions, and hospital carewill be reimbursed by the French government.

Why International Students Need to Register

Registering for French Social Security is generally mandatory for international students staying in France for longer than 3 months. It ensures you have basic health coverage during your studies, helping you avoid out-of-pocket expenses for healthcare.

Who is Eligible?

  • Non-EU/EEA Students: Must register upon arrival to benefit from healthcare coverage.
  • EU/EEA Students: Can usually use their European Health Insurance Card (EHIC). Registration is optional but recommended if staying long-term.
  • Students under 28 from specific countries: Some bilateral agreements exist (e.g., Quebec) which simplify the process.

Documents You'll Typically Need

To register via the dedicated student portal, you usually need to upload:

  • Passport or national ID card
  • Student visa (VLS-TS) and confirmation of validation
  • Proof of university enrollment in France
  • Proof of address in France
  • French Bank Account details (RIB) for reimbursements
  • Full birth certificate (translated into French by a sworn translator if required)

General Registration Process

The primary method for students is to register online at the official foreign student portal (etudiant-etranger.ameli.fr). Once registered, you will receive a temporary Social Security number, followed eventually by a permanent one.

The Carte Vitale

The Carte Vitale is your physical health insurance card. Once you have your permanent Social Security number, you can apply for it online through your Ameli account. Presenting this card at the doctor or pharmacy speeds up your reimbursements directly into your bank account.

Processing Times & Patience

The administrative process can take several months. In the meantime, keep all your feuilles de soins (medical receipts) provided by doctors; you can send them to your local CPAM for reimbursement once you get your temporary number.

Disclaimer: FranceMate provides general guidance and does not make official eligibility or registration decisions. Rules and procedures can change. Always refer to the official ameli.fr website for the most accurate and up-to-date information.
