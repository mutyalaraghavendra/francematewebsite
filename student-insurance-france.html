Insurance

Insurance for International Students in France

Understand common insurance requirements for international students in France, including housing insurance and student-related coverage.

Why Insurance Matters in France

In France, being properly insured is a legal and practical necessity. From renting an apartment to enrolling in a university, you will be required to show proof of various insurances. FranceMate helps you understand these requirements so you can get covered quickly.

Housing Insurance (Assurance Habitation)

Mandatory for Renters: By law, you must have home insurance if you are renting an apartment in France. Your landlord or residence manager will ask for an Attestation d'assurance habitation before giving you the keys.

What it covers: Typical policies cover water damage, fire, explosions, and sometimes burglary or natural disasters. It protects both your belongings and the landlord's property.

Student Civil Liability (ResponsabilitCivile)

Civil liability (Responsabilit Civile) covers accidental damage or injury you might cause to others or their property.

Important: Universities almost always require a certificate of civil liability for enrollment, internships, or field trips. Very often, this coverage is bundled with your housing insurance or your student bank account offer, but it's crucial to verify you have the specific certificate.

Health Coverage Overview

ScuritSociale (Public)

Registration with the French healthcare system is free and mandatory for international students. It reimburses a portion of your medical expenses (usually around 70% for doctor visits). You register at Ameli.fr after arriving.

Mutuelle (Complementary)

A "mutuelle" is a private, optional complementary health insurance. It covers the remaining percentage of medical costs that the ScuritSociale does not cover (like the remaining 30%, or costs for dental and vision care). It is highly recommended.

Travel & Arrival Insurance

Your French public health coverage (ScuritSociale) might take weeks or months to process. For peace of mind, many students purchase short-term travel or private international student health insurance for their first few months in France to ensure full coverage during the transition.

How to Choose Insurance

  • Compare student-focused insurance providers (such as Heyme, SMENO, LMDE) or traditional insurers/banks (MACIF, MAAF, BNP Paribas).
  • Look for bundles: Many providers offer a "Student Pack" combining housing insurance and civil liability at a low monthly rate.
  • Ensure you can get the Attestation (certificate) immediately online to provide to your landlord and university.
