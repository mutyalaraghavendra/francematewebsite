Banking

Bank Account in France for International Students

Learn how international students can prepare to open a French bank account, understand RIB and IBAN, and organize their banking documents.

Why Students Need a French Bank Account

A French bank account is practically mandatory for navigating daily life and administrative tasks in France. You will need it for:

  • CAF (Housing Assistance): The government pays housing aid directly into a French bank account.
  • Paying Rent: Most landlords and agencies require direct debits or transfers from a French account.
  • Mobile & Internet: French telecoms usually demand a French IBAN to set up monthly payments.
  • Student Jobs & Salaries: Employers typically require a French account to pay your wages.
  • Health Insurance Refunds: Social Security reimbursements are sent to your French bank account.

Understanding RIB, IBAN, and BIC

What is a RIB?

RIB stands for Relev d'Identit Bancaire. It's a document provided by your bank that contains all your bank account details. You will frequently be asked to "provide a RIB" when setting up contracts, subscriptions, or payments.

IBAN and BIC/SWIFT

Your RIB includes your IBAN (International Bank Account Number, starting with FR in France) and your BIC/SWIFT code. The IBAN is what you share to receive transfers or authorize direct debits (prlvements).

Common Documents Needed to Open an Account

To open a bank account in France, you will generally need to provide:

  • Valid ID: Passport and your French Visa (or Carte de Sjour).
  • Proof of Address (Justificatif de domicile): A recent utility bill, internet bill, or rent receipt in your name. (See below if you don't have this yet.)
  • Proof of Student Status: Your university enrollment certificate (Attestation d'inscription) or student ID card.
  • Tax Identification: Sometimes your tax number from your home country is requested for international compliance.

Handling Address Requirements

A common Catch-22 is that you need a bank account to rent an apartment, but an address to open a bank account. If you are staying in temporary housing, you may need an Attestation d'hbergement (a signed letter from your host, along with their ID and utility bill) to serve as your proof of address.

Types of Banks in France

  • Traditional Banks: Physical branches, personal advisors. Examples: BNP Paribas, Socit Gnrale, LCL, Crdit Agricole. Often have student offers but can have slower administrative processes.
  • Online Banks & Neobanks: Digital-first banks. Examples: Boursorama (BoursoBank), Hello bank!, N26, Revolut. Faster to open, lower fees, but some may have limitations regarding French IBANs or depositing cash.
  • La Banque Postale: The postal bank. It's accessible and has branches everywhere, but administrative times can vary.

Banking Terminology in French

  • Compte Courant: Checking/Current account.
  • Carte Bancaire (CB): Debit or credit card.
  • Virement: Bank transfer.
  • Prlvement: Direct debit.
  • Dcouvert: Overdraft.
  • Frais bancaires: Bank fees.
