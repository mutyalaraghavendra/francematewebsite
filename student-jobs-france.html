Where to Look

Paris Areas With Student Job Opportunities

Knowing where to look is half the battle. These areas in Paris are well known for student-friendly employer density.

La Dfense

Major business district. Office support, catering, retail in the shopping centre (Les 4 Temps). Good for formal part-time roles.

ChteletLes Halles

Central Paris hub. Dense with fast food, retail stores, restaurants and cafs. High turnover means frequent openings.

Saint-Denis / Stade de France

Large student population, developing commercial areas. Lower competition for jobs. Close to several universities.

Montparnasse

Tourist-facing area with hotels, restaurants and shops. Good for hospitality roles. Accessible from multiple Metro lines.

Clichy / Bois-Colombes

Industrial and logistics zones near Paris. Good for warehouse, inventory and delivery-type roles. Flexible shift patterns.

Your University Campus

Campus jobs (library, cafeteria, student services) are ideal flexible, understand your academic schedule, and require less French.

How to Approach

How to Approach an Employer in Paris

Many student jobs in Paris are found by walking in and asking directly not just applying online. Here's how to do it right.

1

Prepare your documents

Bring printed copies of your CV (in French), your student card, your passport and your titre de sjour / visa. Some employers ask on the spot.

2

Go in person, not by phone

In France, walking into a cafor shop with your CV and asking politely is well-accepted. Go during quiet hours avoid lunch rush (122pm) and dinner service (79pm).

3

Start with a polite greeting

Always say "Bonjour Madame / Monsieur" first. Then: "Je cherche un emploi tudiant temps partiel est-ce que vous recrutez?"
("I'm looking for a part-time student job are you hiring?")

4

Hand over your CV

Even if they say no, leave your CV. Managers often keep CVs and call when someone leaves. Always smile and say "Merci, bonne journe" as you leave.

5

Follow up after 1 week

If you don't hear back, return in person or call to ask if they've had a chance to review your application. Persistence (without being annoying) is respected.

Important to Know

Work Rules for International Students in France

Hour Limits (Non-EU Students)

If you hold a French student visa (VLS-TS), non-EU students are generally permitted to work up to 964 hours per year approximately 20 hours per week. Always verify your specific visa conditions as rules can vary.

What You May Need

Employers may ask for: your titre de sjour (residence permit), passport, French bank account details (RIB), a numro de scurit sociale, and a justificatif de domicile (proof of address).
