SIM Card

SIM Cards in France for International Students

Moving to France as a student? Learn how to choose a French SIM or eSIM, compare mobile options, and get connected right after arrival.

Why You Need a French Number

Getting a French mobile number (+33) is one of the first and most critical steps when you arrive in France. You will need it for:

  • Administration: Opening a bank account, registering for Social Security, and applying for CAF all require a French number.
  • Housing & Services: Landlords, agencies, and delivery drivers will expect to call a local number.
  • Daily Life: Communicating with university peers, navigating with Google Maps, and using apps like WhatsApp smoothly.

SIM vs eSIM

Physical SIM

The traditional plastic card. You can purchase these at airports, tobacco shops (tabacs), supermarkets, or order them online. Suitable for any unlocked phone.

eSIM

A digital SIM embedded in your phone. It allows you to activate a plan instantly without physical delivery. Very convenient, but you must ensure your smartphone is eSIM-compatible (like recent iPhones, Pixels, and Galaxy devices).

Choosing a Mobile Plan (Forfait Mobile)

Mobile plans in France are generally very affordable and generous with data compared to many other countries. You have a few main options:

  • No-contract Plans (Forfait sans engagement): Highly recommended for students. You pay monthly but can cancel anytime without penalty.
  • Prepaid Cards (Cartes prpayes): Good for the first few days, but more expensive in the long run and often provide less data.
  • Data considerations: Most students opt for plans ranging from 50GB to 200GB, which cost between 10 to 20 per month.

France has several major network operators and various low-cost subsidiary brands that offer excellent "sans engagement" deals online.

Documents Needed

To sign up for a monthly mobile plan online, you typically need:

  • A valid ID (Passport or ID card)
  • A French delivery address (for physical SIMs)
  • A European bank card or a French bank account (RIB) to set up direct debit payments (prlvement).

Getting Connected After Landing

If you need connectivity immediately upon arriving at Paris CDG or Orly:

  • Airport Relay/Tabac stores: You can buy basic prepaid SIM cards here, though they can be pricey.
  • eSIMs: If your phone is compatible, you can purchase a travel eSIM before you even fly, ensuring you have data the moment you land. Later, you can switch to a local French plan.
